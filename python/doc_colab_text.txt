Richard Aragon
AI University
  

Abstract This paper introduces Quantum Geometry Learning Systems (QGLS), a novel AI architecture that integrates the structural principles of quantum mechanics into classical neural networks via topological design. The architecture simulates entanglement, interference, and wavefunction collapse through knot-based node arrangements and entangled signal propagation. QGLS presents a new paradigm in AI: leveraging the shape and resonance behavior of quantum systems to optimize learning dynamics, generalization, and information propagation without requiring quantum hardware.
1. Introduction Contemporary neural networks rely on feedforward layers, convolutional filters, and transformer attention mechanisms. These models are effective but ultimately limited by linear data flow and layer-bound signal routing. QGLS departs from this paradigm, utilizing quantum geometry — the topological characteristics of quantum mechanical systems — to define a new learning substrate. QGLS builds networks shaped like physical knot structures (e.g., trefoil, figure-eight) and replaces traditional linear propagation with entangled and resonant signal flow.
2. Core Principles of QGLS
* Topology as Intelligence Substrate: Nodes are arranged in 3D knot structures, forming a resonant, entangled network topology.
* Entangled Connection Layer: Each connection has entanglement coefficient (ε), resonance phase (ϕ), and knot tension (τ), creating complex signal modulation analogous to quantum interference.
* Wave-Based Propagation: Signals propagate through entangled paths, dynamically interfering and reinforcing in a manner similar to quantum wavefunctions.
* Collapse Resolution Layer: Propagated superposition states are resolved into decisions using entropy, energy, or tension-based collapse methods.
* Resonance Regularization: Phase harmony is enforced through a custom loss function that penalizes disharmonic interactions across the network.
3. Architecture Overview
* TopologyGenerator: Defines the knot-based node layout and connection paths.
* DatasetAdapter: Projects input data onto the knot structure, mapping information topologically.
* EntangledConnectionLayer: Modulates signals using entanglement and resonance.
* EntanglementPropagator: Simulates wave propagation across the network.
* CollapseResolutionLayer: Resolves distributed signals into output predictions.
* ResonanceLoss and TopologicalRegularizer: Guide model learning toward harmonic equilibrium and structural consistency.
4. Experimental Setup The QGLS model was tested on the Fashion MNIST dataset. Initial results demonstrate competitive performance compared to traditional convolutional models, with notable improvements in signal coherence, learning stability, and generalization under noise. The model was trained with standard optimization protocols while monitoring entanglement dynamics and topological tension.
5. Implications and Future Work QGLS suggests that intelligence may emerge more efficiently in systems shaped by the geometric and resonant principles of quantum mechanics. Future work includes extending the architecture to dynamic topology adjustment, memory integration via persistent homology, and application to temporal/spatial data domains. Additionally, QGLS opens a path to a new class of AI models shaped by fundamental physics rather than layered abstraction.
6. Conclusion Quantum Geometry Learning Systems represent a shift in AI thinking — from linear computation to resonant topological intelligence. By shaping classical systems in the form of quantum geometries, QGLS bridges the gap between physics and machine learning, revealing new dimensions in computational structure and signal flow.
