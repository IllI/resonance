# Unveiling the Ghost Basin: Zero-Shot Categorical Decoding from Interference-Distorted fMRI Signals via Unsupervised Latent Clustering

## 1. Introduction

Categorical perception in the human brain is highly idiosyncratic, characterized by anatomical variability, cognitive fatigue, and attentional drift. Traditional neuroimaging decoding models typically rely on aligning physical voxel spaces (e.g., Shared Response Modeling) or training rigid classifiers. These methods often fail during zero-shot inference on unseen subjects because they assume a direct, consistent mapping between biological anatomy and cognitive state. However, categories themselves are abstract representations—universal "superpositions"—whose orientational geometry exists independently of a singular, momentary biological instantiation. 

We hypothesize that an individual's brain activation pattern in response to a stimulus is not the pure category itself, but rather a noisy projection towards this Universal Superposition, heavily distorted by transient, state-dependent interference. Rather than forcing diverse human anatomies into a single shared physical grid, we propose mapping these temporally dynamic signals into an invariant semantic latent space. By analyzing the topological clusters of these mapped thoughts, we can isolate the general shape and orientation of the abstract concepts, effectively canceling out the subject-specific noise to decode the unanalyzed brain.

## 2. Mathematical Formulation of the Generative Basin

Let $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ be the observed multi-voxel brain activation pattern over time $T$ across $N$ voxels in response to a given stimulus. 

We define a set of ideal, invariant categorical superpositions $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_K\}$, where each $s_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ represents the pure mathematical centroid of a category (e.g., "Face", "House") in a highly dimensional semantic latent space.

We train an alignment mapping function (such as an LSTM operating on sparse auto-encoded features) $f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{T \times N} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^D$. For a well-trained network recognizing a known brain state, $f_\theta(Y) \approx s_k$. 

However, during **zero-shot inference on an unanalyzed candidate**, the mapped brain pattern $z_i = f_\theta(Y_i)$ in response to a true target concept $s^*$ is distorted. We define this generative observation as:

$$ z_i = s^* + \epsilon_i $$

where $\epsilon_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ represents the **Interference Term**. This term encapsulates the cross-talk generated by the subject's unique anatomy, cognitive exhaustion, and wandering attention at the specific moment $t$ the stimulus was presented.

## 3. Solving for the Superposition

Because the interference vector $\epsilon_i$ is highly variable and unpredictable, relying on a single zero-shot prediction ($z_i \approx s_k$) is fundamentally brittle. A single brain wave response does not neatly encapsulate the superposition. 

To solve for the true categorical shape, we must traverse the data by characterizing multiple activation responses inspired by the stimuli. We establish a set of unknown zero-shot observations $Z = \{z_1, z_2, \dots, z_M\}$. 

We apply an unsupervised partitioning algorithm (e.g., $K$-means) to group these activation patterns into distinct camps $C_1, \dots, C_K$ based purely on their geometric proximities in the latent space. 

For any given camp $C$, its morphological centroid $\mu_C$ is calculated as the mean orientation of all thoughts contained within it:

$$ \mu_C = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{z_i \in C} z_i $$

By geometrically averaging the set, we leverage the Law of Large Numbers to cancel out the zero-mean random interference specific to that subject's temporary mental states:

$$ \mu_C = \frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{z_i \in C} (s^* + \epsilon_i) \approx s^* + \mathbb{E}[\epsilon] \approx s^* $$

## 4. Recovering the Unknown Category

Once the distinct camps of activation patterns are characterized and the general shape of the individual's "Ghost Basin" $\mu_C$ is established, we solve for the unknown stimulus category by estimating the mathematical distance to the predetermined models of categorical shapes.

The predicted category $\hat{c}$ for the entire unanalyzed cluster is defined by the maximum cosine similarity to the known Universal Superpositions $s_k \in S$:

$$ \hat{c} = \arg\max_{s_k \in S} \frac{\mu_C \cdot s_k}{\|\mu_C\|_2 \|s_k\|_2} $$

By clustering the expressions prior to classification, we transition from analyzing brittle, distorted individual perceptions to measuring the converged, generalized orientation of the brain's conceptual target. This unsupervised characterization theoretically bridges the gap between individual biological variance and universal abstract perception.
