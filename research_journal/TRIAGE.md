# Scientific Triage: Candidate Diamonds and Hard Negative Results

This is an audit queue, not a publication claim. Scores and evidence grades come from `FAMILY_CATALOG.csv`.

## Tier A — audit first

1. **IBM `T_xx` witness curve across two layouts (E4).** Raw counts and replicated nulls are the strongest evidence package in the repository. The defensible result is narrow: one PTM component follows an attenuated analytic curve; full rank collapse and teleportation were not measured. Novelty versus existing OAT/channel literature needs an external literature review.

2. **Exact PTM correction and `chi*t=pi` null (E3).** The analytic/numerical correction chain is foundational and unusually transparent. It may support a methods/theory paper when paired with the hardware witness, provided the proof and all fidelity conventions are independently audited.

3. **Blind invariant-spectrum stratification, Program F (E3).** Adversarial orientation and separable controls make this the strongest D-LinOSS result. The likely contribution is representation-invariant operator morphology, not source identification or quantum emergence.

4. **Representation fronts, Programs I/I+ (E3).** The separation between mutual-information/operator-spreading propagation and organized recoverability—especially the Ising null—is scientifically meaningful. Replication beyond `N=10` for the full four-representation comparison could turn this into a clean conceptual result.

5. **Program O preregistration contradiction (E2).** Large W3/OAT endpoint gains oppose the expected regime hierarchy, and the subject miscounts 57/60 versus the JSON's 51/60. This is a high-surprise replication target, not yet a result to promote.

## Tier B — promising after evidence repair

- **Recovery-basin geometry (`V_Q`).** Potentially reusable order-parameter framework, but exponent and stiffness claims were optimizer-sensitive and partly retracted.
- **SO(3) kernel–Galerkin TPU study.** Restricted-regime convergence is numerically interesting; full asymptotics failed under float32 conditioning. Requires mathematical review and higher-precision replication.
- **Tunnel-eigen AP-U calibration.** Locking random/fold-in keys cleanly demonstrates how calibration drift can manufacture controller wins. Raw production artifacts should be recovered.
- **MPS plus linear SSM capacity result.** A live follow-up reportedly crosses its recovery gate while D-LinOSS does not. It remains uncommitted and must be rerun.
- **Page–Wootters synthetic bridge.** Clean relational alignment is reproducible in the generator, but noise robustness, path leakage, and post-hoc threshold revision are unresolved.

## High-value negative/closeout results

- Chronos sequencing, replication, and time-shift controls favor runtime/extractor structure over synchronized external timing.
- MERA Programs X–AA improve observability without beneficial intervention.
- AJ's forecast-horizon sweep is actually an all-gates-saturated controller control.
- AQ's payload bug invalidates earlier operator-identifiability claims and narrows the durable result to tiny predefined source/motif recovery.
- TRAPPIST's pipeline-stage audit invalidates earlier astrophysical sensitivity interpretations and favors simpler baselines.
- GLIMPSE/LRD lacks a validated clock axis and matched controls; physical-history claims are blocked.

## Plausible paper clusters

1. **Reduced-state PTM structure and a replicated hardware witness** — exact identities, corrected recovery conventions, raw IBM counts, tightly bounded claims.
2. **Information propagation is not recoverability** — Programs H/I/J/K, emphasizing representation-specific fronts and failed strict causal precedence.
3. **Failure-aware adaptive control** — abstention, inactive triggers, saturation, key leakage, and preregistration contradictions as a benchmark/methodology paper.
4. **Numerical operator morphology with D-LinOSS** — Program F as the center, with explicit adversarial limits and no quantum-emergence rhetoric.

The first paper cluster currently has the strongest evidentiary spine. The third may be the most broadly reusable if reconstructed carefully.
